# 7715: deacons

Those who “serve” in the Christian community, assisting the elders or overseers (bishops). The Greek word which the NIV translates four times as “deacon” occurs a further 25 times in the NT, where it is translated as “servant”. It seems that all Christians have a general servant role, whereas some (deacons) are called to a specific office of service within the church.

## The Seven: assistants to the apostles


### Why they were needed
**[ACT 6:1-6:4](bibleref:44006001-44006004)** *The verb “wait” means “to serve”.* 

### Their qualifications and selection
**[ACT 6:3](bibleref:44006003)**

### Their names and appointment
**[ACT 6:5-6:6](bibleref:44006005-44006006)** *Although the Seven are not actually called “deacons”, they were probably the earliest example of Christians carrying out the tasks which came to be linked with deacons.* 

## Deacons were assistants to overseers in Philippi
**[PHP 1:1](bibleref:50001001)** *All Christians are “saints” (that is, set apart for God), but overseers and deacons have specific roles assigned to them.* 

## Qualifications for men and women deacons
**[1TI 3:8-3:13](bibleref:54003008-54003013)** *There is no NT description of the precise role of deacons (although Ac 6:1-4 may imply a certain role). These qualifications would be appropriate for people responsible for financial management, administration, and social service, such as that offered to widows* ; **[1TI 3:11](bibleref:54003011)** *The women may be deacons' wives (NIV text), or deaconesses (NIV footnote).*
