# 5618: weights and measures, linear

These terms were based on natural units of measurement that could be easily applied. Measures are given in ascending order, with their approximate imperial and metric equivalents.



## Finger (1/4 handbreadth): about 3/4 inch (about 1.85 centimetres)
**JER 52:21** *The finger was 1/4 handbreadth and was the smallest subdivision of the cubit. This is its only mention in Scripture.*

## Handbreadth (4 fingers): about 3 inches (about 8 centimetres)
**EXO 25:25** parallel EXO 37:12 *The handbreadth was the width of the hand at the base of the four fingers.* *See also* 1KI 7:26 parallel 2CH 4:5; PSA 39:5 *The handbreadth is here used figuratively of the shortness of human life*; EZK 40:5

## Span (half a cubit): about 9 inches (about 23 centimetres)
**EXO 28:16** parallel EXO 39:9 *The span was the width of the outstretched hand from thumb to little finger.* *See also* 1SA 17:4 *Goliath's height is given in the Hebrew as “six cubits and a span”*; PSA 90:10 *The span is here used figuratively of the shortness of human life*; EZK 43:13

## Cubit (2 spans): about 18 inches (about 0.5 metre)
**EXO 25:10** parallel EXO 37:1 *The cubit was the distance from the fingertip to the elbow, and was used to measure height, size, depth and distance.* *See also* GEN 7:20 *The Hebrew describes the depth of the water as “fifteen cubits”*; EXO 27:9-27:18 parallel EXO 38:9-38:15; 1KI 6:2-6:3 parallel 2CH 3:3-3:4; 1CH 11:23 *The Hebrew describes the man as “five cubits tall”*; EZK 40:5, EZK 45:1-45:6, EZK 47:3-47:5; JHN 21:8 *The Greek text describes the distance as “about two hundred cubits”*; REV 21:17

## Reed (6 cubits): about 10 feet (about 3 metres)
**EZK 41:8** *Originally a measuring instrument, the reed became a recognised measure of six cubits (the rod).* *See also* EZK 40:3, EZK 40:5-40:7; REV 11:1, REV 21:15-21:16

## Mile
**MAT 5:41** *The Greek word for “mile” here is “milion”, a transliteration of the Roman measurement “mille passuum”, “a thousand paces”.* *See also* LUK 24:13 *The measurement in the Greek is “sixty stadia”. Eight stadia were about one mile (about 1480 metres)*; JHN 6:19 *The measurement in the Greek is “twenty-five or thirty stadia”*; JHN 11:18 *The measurement in the Greek is “fifteen stadia”.*
